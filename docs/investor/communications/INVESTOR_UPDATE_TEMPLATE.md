# Nodbusiness — Monthly Investor Update Template

> **Classification:** Confidential
> **Purpose:** Send this to all investors and key advisors every month.
> Consistent updates build trust, keep investors engaged, and often lead
> to introductions, advice, and follow-on investment.
>
> **When to send:** First week of the following month (e.g., August update sent Sept 1–5)
> **Who receives it:** All SAFE holders, shareholders, board members, key advisors
> **Format:** Email + optionally a PDF attachment

---

## Acronyms Used in This Document

| Acronym | Full Form |
|---------|-----------|
| MAU | Monthly Active Users — unique users active at least once per month |
| MRR | Monthly Recurring Revenue — total subscription revenue in one month |
| SAFE | Simple Agreement for Future Equity — pre-seed investment instrument |
| KPI | Key Performance Indicator — measurable value tracking business goal progress |
| MoM | Month over Month — comparing a metric to the previous month |
| D7 | Day 7 Retention — percentage of users still active 7 days after registration |
| D30 | Day 30 Retention — percentage of users still active 30 days after registration |
| CAC | Customer Acquisition Cost — total cost to acquire one paying customer |

---

## Why Monthly Updates Matter

```
Most founders SKIP investor updates. This is a huge mistake.

Investors who receive regular updates:
  → Trust you more (you're transparent, not hiding problems)
  → Are more likely to make introductions
  → Are more likely to invest in the next round
  → Will support you in board votes
  → Will give you advice you didn't know you needed

Investors who DON'T receive updates:
  → Forget about you
  → Assume things are bad (silence = problems)
  → Are harder to reach when you actually need help
  → Are less enthusiastic in future fundraising
```

## The Update Structure (Keep It Under 500 Words)

```
Subject line: Nodbusiness Update — [Month Year] | [One key metric]

Example: "Nodbusiness Update — August 2026 | 2,847 MAU (+18%)"

Why this subject line works:
  → Immediately shows progress (the key metric)
  → Investors scan dozens of emails — yours stands out with a number
  → Even if they don't read the full update, they see you're growing
```

## What Makes a Great Update vs. a Bad One

| Great Update | Bad Update |
|-------------|------------|
| Specific numbers ("4,200 MAU") | Vague language ("good growth") |
| Honest about challenges | Only talks about wins |
| Short (under 500 words) | Long essay nobody finishes |
| Specific ask ("intro to X") | No ask or vague ask ("any help appreciated") |
| Sent consistently every month | Sent randomly or only when things are good |
| Shows momentum even if numbers are small | Hides slow periods |
| Proactively addresses big problems | Waits until investors ask |

## Investor Communication Rules

```
1. Send every month — even if numbers are bad
   → "We had a tough month — here's what happened and what we're doing"
   → This builds MORE trust than only updating when things are good

2. Reply to all investor emails within 24 hours
   → Even if just: "Got it — will respond fully by [date]"

3. Never surprise investors with bad news
   → If something major happens (key person leaves, technical issue, legal),
     tell investors BEFORE it becomes public
   → "I wanted to let you know directly before you hear elsewhere..."

4. Ask for specific help — not general support
   → "Can you introduce me to [specific person]?" converts much better than
     "Let me know if you can help with anything"

5. Celebrate investors publicly (when they want it)
   → LinkedIn post: "Excited to announce [investor] joined our pre-seed round"
   → Investors like public credit — it helps them too
   → Always ask permission before announcing an investment publicly

6. Share wins that aren't in the numbers
   → "A customer sent this message..." (screenshot)
   → "Our first international user signed up from [country]..."
   → Human stories make investors feel connected to the mission
```
