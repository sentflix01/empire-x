## Category 1 — The Market

**Q: How big is the market?**
```
TAM: $24B global SMB website builder + marketplace market.
SAM: $1.2B for Africa + diaspora, growing at 28% CAGR.
SOM: $12M ARR is our 5-year capture target — 1% of SAM.

The more compelling number: 400 million SMBs globally have no digital presence.
We are the first platform that makes it free and easy to have one.
```

**Q: Why Africa? Why not start in a bigger market?**
```
Three reasons:
1. First-mover advantage: No platform owns this space in Africa yet.
   Shopify, Wix, and Squarespace have <5% market penetration in Africa.

2. Network effects work faster in underserved markets: Each business we add
   brings their customers. In a market with no existing solution, adoption
   accelerates faster than in saturated markets.

3. We understand this market: We know the local payment methods (Telebirr,
   M-Pesa), the language, the business culture, and the specific pain points.

Our global strategy: Africa first, then diaspora communities worldwide,
then full global expansion via developer marketplace (Phase 3).
```

**Q: What's your unfair advantage against a US competitor entering Africa?**
```
Four moats:

1. Local payment integration: Telebirr, CBE Birr, M-Pesa — these take
   18+ months to integrate properly. We did it. Shopify hasn't.

2. Local trust and culture: Businesses trust a platform built by and for
   their market. A US company entering needs years to build this trust.

3. Network effects already in motion: 500 showrooms creates a discovery
   network. A new entrant starts at zero.

4. Price point: We can price for the market. A US company with US cost
   structures cannot profitably serve $19/month African customers.
```

## Category 2 — The Product

**Q: What problem does Nodbusiness solve that WhatsApp doesn't already solve?**
```
WhatsApp solves communication. It doesn't solve discovery.

A business on WhatsApp can message existing customers.
But: New customers cannot FIND that business. There is no searchable directory.
There is no catalog. There is no payment integration. There is no professional presence.

Nodbusiness solves the discovery problem: Any customer anywhere can find
a business, see their full product catalog, read reviews, and pay —
without ever needing the business's WhatsApp number.

WhatsApp is a messaging tool. Nodbusiness is a business platform.
They complement each other — we integrate with WhatsApp for notifications.
```

**Q: How is this different from a basic website builder?**
```
Five differences:

1. Discovery layer: A Wix website lives at a URL nobody visits.
   A Nodbusiness showroom is discovered through a social feed, nearby map,
   and search — just like Instagram stories or Google Maps, but for businesses.

2. Community: Users follow businesses, comment, share, and engage.
   No traditional website builder has a social layer.

3. Commerce integration: Orders, payments, delivery tracking — all built in.
   Wix charges extra for each capability.

4. Mobile-first: Built for phone-only users (no laptop required to build or use).
   Traditional builders assume a laptop.

5. Integrated tasks + gigs: Users can post tasks and hire from the same platform
   where they buy products and follow businesses. No other platform does this.
```

**Q: Why won't Facebook or Instagram just add this feature?**
```
They might. This is a real risk. Our response:

1. They tried and failed: Facebook Marketplace and Instagram Shopping exist.
   They don't solve the "build a professional website" problem.
   They show products in a feed — not a full showroom/website experience.

2. We're faster: By the time they build this (18–24 months minimum),
   we'll have network effects, developer ecosystem, and data moat.

3. Trust: Small businesses in Africa don't trust Meta after years of
   algorithm changes killing organic reach. We're building trust by design.

4. Regulation risk: Meta faces regulatory scrutiny globally. 
   A local platform has less regulatory overhead.

The real question is: can we build a strong enough moat before they copy?
We believe yes — 3 patents, developer ecosystem, local payment integration.
```

## Category 3 — Business Model

**Q: Why would someone pay $29–$99/month when free alternatives exist?**
```
Same reason 4 million businesses pay Shopify $29–$299/month
when they could use a free website.

Free tier gives enough to attract users and prove value.
Paid tier removes limitations (branding, products, analytics) that
growth-oriented businesses hit within 30–90 days.

Key insight: Our target customer is not "I want a website."
Our target customer is "I want customers." They'll pay for discovery and sales.

Evidence: We have [X] businesses on our waitlist for paid access
before we've even launched the paid tier.
[Update with real number when available]
```

**Q: How do you prevent businesses from building their showroom then canceling?**
```
High switching cost architecture:
1. All customer relationships live on Nodbusiness (followers, order history, reviews)
2. Published showroom URL is indexed by search engines — losing it hurts SEO
3. Developer templates earn revenue monthly — template developers create
   ongoing value that incentivizes staying subscribed
4. Integrated payments: switching means rebuilding payment integration
5. Customer base: their customers are used to finding them on Nodbusiness

Our target churn: <3% monthly (industry average for SMB SaaS: 2–5%)
Payback period: 1.2 months (making churn economics very forgiving)
```

**Q: What is your customer acquisition cost (CAC)?**
```
Phase 1: $0 CAC (organic only)
Mechanism: Every business brings their own customers to the platform.
Each customer then discovers other businesses organically.
Viral coefficient target: 1.5 (each user brings 1.5 more)

Phase 2 blended CAC target: $30/business
Channels: App store (ASO), LinkedIn ads, referral program

LTV at $29/month × 24-month average lifetime = $696
LTV:CAC = 696/30 = 23:1 (industry benchmark: >3:1 is good, 23:1 is excellent)
```

## Category 4 — Competition

**Q: What happens when Shopify decides to build a social layer?**
```
We will have a 5-year head start on network effects.

But more importantly: Our architecture is fundamentally different.
Shopify is built around inventory management → checkout → payments.
Nodbusiness is built around social graph → discovery → commerce.

These are different architectural philosophies. Shopify adding a "follow"
button doesn't make them Nodbusiness — it requires rebuilding their core loop.

Also: Shopify's smallest plan is $29/month, requires a laptop to set up,
and has no social discovery layer. Our free tier beats their paid tier
for the businesses we serve.
```

**Q: Are you worried about local copycats?**
```
Copycats are evidence of market validation — not a threat.

What protects us:
1. Network effects: First platform to 10,000 users wins. That's our focus.
2. Patents: 3 provisional applications for our core innovations.
3. Developer ecosystem: 50+ template developers will be invested in our
   success. They don't switch — their revenue switches too.
4. Data moat: Our AI pricing improves with more data. A copycat starts cold.

We welcome competition. It means the market is real.
```

**Q: What do you do if this company fails?**
```
This is a trap question. There is only one right answer:

"I'm not thinking about failure. I'm thinking about what I need to do
to make this succeed. The only scenario I'm planning for is success."

(Then briefly mention resilience if pushed: "I've faced [relevant challenge]
before. I know how to adapt and push through difficult periods.")
```

**Q: Have you done this before?**
```

"This is my first company. But I've [relevant experience].
More importantly, I've already proven execution — this platform is built
and working. That matters more than previous startups."

If yes: Share specifically what you learned from previous ventures.
```

## Category 6 — Financials & Risk

**Q: What's your biggest risk?**
```
Honest answer builds trust. Pick the real risk, not a fake weakness.

"Our biggest risk is user retention in Phase 1.
If businesses publish a showroom but don't see customer traffic in the
first 30 days, they'll churn before we can prove value.

We're mitigating this by:
1. Mandatory 'launch boost' — every new showroom gets featured in the
   discovery feed for 7 days (free promotional push)
2. Email sequence: Day 1, 3, 7, 14 — tips to drive showroom traffic
3. 'First sale guarantee' — we personally help the first 50 businesses
   get their first customer through the platform

This is the bet: if we nail early retention, the rest follows."
```

**Q: What if you can't raise a Series A after this seed?**
```
"We won't need to.

At $25,000 MRR (our Series A trigger), our gross margin is 82%.
At that run rate, we have positive contribution margin.
With our burn rate of $21,500/month and $1M seed, we have 46 months runway.

By Month 18, we should be approaching cash-flow positive.
Series A is a choice to accelerate, not a survival requirement."
```

**Q: How do you handle currency risk? (Revenue in ETB, costs in USD)**
```
"We price in local currency and USD simultaneously.

Subscription plans priced in both:
  → Ethiopian customers: ETB-denominated (protected from currency risk)
  → International customers: USD-denominated (our natural hedge)

As we expand globally, USD revenue grows as a percentage of total —
which means currency risk DECREASES as we scale.

Short term, we hold minimal ETB reserves, converting to USD promptly
after payment processing."
```

## The Golden Rules for Investor Meetings

```
Before the meeting:
  → Research the investor: know their portfolio, their thesis, their background
  → Know your numbers cold: MAU, MRR, CAC, LTV, churn, runway, growth rate
  → Prepare a specific ask from THIS investor (intro, advice, investment)

During the meeting:
  → Listen more than you talk in the first 10 minutes
  → Answer directly — never give a non-answer
  → Show genuine excitement (investors bet on passionate founders)
  → If you don't know something: "I don't know, I'll find out by [date]"
  → End with: "Based on what you've heard, do you have any concerns?"

After the meeting:
  → Send a thank-you email within 24 hours
  → Include: what you discussed, what you'll send them, next step
  → Follow up on every promise within the timeline you committed
  → Ask: "Is there anyone else you think I should talk to?"

Never:
  → Lie or exaggerate metrics — investors do reference checks
  → Be defensive about concerns — "That's a great concern. Here's how we think about it..."
  → Give up equity verbally without written documentation
  → Discuss terms before they've seen the full pitch
```
