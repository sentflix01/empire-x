## 1. The Principle: Raise When You Have Leverage

The single biggest mistake founders make is raising money when they are desperate.
Investors can sense desperation. It destroys your negotiating power.

**The right time to raise:**
- You have proven users or revenue (even small)
- You have a clear use of funds
- You are not in immediate financial crisis
- You have multiple investors interested (creates competition)

**Halehame's current position:**
- Platform is built (strong technical foundation)
- 0 paying users (no traction yet — early stage)
- Seed round appropriate — but build traction first

## 2. Pre-Seed Round ($50K–$150K)

### What Is Pre-Seed?
The very first money into the company, before you have significant users or revenue.
It comes from people who bet on YOU and the idea, not the metrics.

### Who to Approach (Priority Order)

**1. Friends & Family**
People who know and trust you personally.
They invest in you, not the business metrics.
```
Typical amount: $5,000–$50,000 per person
Equity to offer: 1%–5% per investor
Structure: SAFE note (see SAFE_AGREEMENT_GUIDE.md)
Advantage: Low scrutiny, fast process, flexible terms
Risk: Damages relationships if company fails
```

**2. Angel Investors (Ethiopia & Diaspora)**
High-net-worth individuals who invest their own money.
Ethiopian diaspora angels are ideal — they understand the market.
```
Where to find them:
  → Ethiopian diaspora business networks (USA, Europe, Middle East)
  → LinkedIn: search "angel investor Ethiopia" or "startup investor Africa"
  → Y Combinator's Startup School network
  → AfriLabs (network of African startup hubs)
  → Addis Ababa Chamber of Commerce connections
Typical amount: $25,000–$200,000 per angel
Equity to offer: 5%–15% total in pre-seed
Structure: SAFE note or convertible note
```

**3. African-Focused Micro-VCs**

## 5. How to Get an Investor's Attention

### Step 1 — Build the right materials first
Before approaching anyone:
- [ ] Pitch deck (see PITCH_DECK_OUTLINE.md)
- [ ] Executive summary (1 page, see EXECUTIVE_SUMMARY.md)
- [ ] Financial projections (3 years, see FINANCIAL_PROJECTIONS.md)
- [ ] Demo video (2–3 minutes, shows the product working)
- [ ] Data room (shared folder with all due diligence materials)

### Step 2 — Warm introductions beat cold emails
```
Success rate comparison:
  Cold email to investor:          < 1% response rate
  Introduction from founder they funded: 40–60% response rate
  Introduction from advisor they respect: 25–40% response rate

How to get warm intros:
  → Apply to accelerators (YC, Techstars) — they make intros
  → Attend startup events (AfriLabs, Demo Africa, Seedstars)
  → Build relationships with advisors who know investors
  → Join AngelList: angel.co — direct investor access
  → Use LinkedIn mutual connections
```

### Step 3 — The 3-email rule
If you must cold email:
```
Email 1: Subject line: "[Company] — [One sentence traction metric]"
  Body: 3 sentences maximum.
  Sentence 1: What you do and for whom.
  Sentence 2: Your best traction number.
  Sentence 3: Ask for 20-minute call.

Example:
  "Halehame lets any business build a professional website in 10 minutes.
  We have 500 published showrooms and 3,000 MAU after 60 days.
  Would you have 20 minutes this week to learn more?"
```

### Step 4 — Investor meeting protocol
```
First meeting:  Understand their thesis, tell your story, do NOT ask for money yet
Second meeting: Deep dive on product, market, team
Third meeting:  Commercial terms discussion
Term sheet:     Negotiate, sign, close
```

## 6. What to Say No To

```
❌ Never give away >25% in a single round — you need room for future rounds
❌ Never accept investor board control (majority board seats)
❌ Never accept full ratchet anti-dilution (use broad-based weighted average)
❌ Never promise specific exit timelines to investors
❌ Never take investment with punitive liquidation preferences (>1× non-participating)
❌ Never accept terms you don't fully understand without an attorney
❌ Never close an investor you haven't thoroughly vetted (bad investors are worse than no investors)
```
