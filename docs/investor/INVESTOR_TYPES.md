## Type 1 — Friends & Family

### Who They Are
People who know and trust you personally. They invest in YOU, not the business.

### How to Approach
```
1. Have a clear, honest conversation about the risk:
   "I need $20,000 to build my company. There is a real chance I lose this money.
    Please only invest what you can afford to never see again."

2. Use proper legal documents — even with family:
   → SAFE note (see SAFE_AGREEMENT_GUIDE.md)
   → Prevents future misunderstandings
   → Shows professionalism

3. Set clear expectations:
   → "You will not see returns for 5–7 years minimum"
   → "You will not have input into business decisions"
   → "I will send monthly updates"

4. Keep amounts reasonable:
   → $5,000–$25,000 per person is typical
   → Never take someone's life savings
```

### Pros / Cons
```
Pros: Fast, low scrutiny, supportive
Cons: Damages relationships if company fails, they expect updates
```

## Type 2 — Angel Investors

### Who They Are
High-net-worth individuals (HNWIs) who invest their own money.
Usually successful entrepreneurs or executives who want to give back + earn returns.

### Types of Angels

**Solo Angels**
Invest alone. Make their own decisions. No committee approval needed.
Best for: Fast closes, simple terms

**Syndicate Angels**
Lead investor organizes a group via AngelList, Republic, or Syndicate platforms.
The lead does due diligence; followers invest smaller amounts.
Best for: Raising $100K–$500K from many small checks at once

**Super Angels**
Angel investors who act like institutional VCs.
Invest $200K–$1M from their own funds. More rigorous but bigger checks.
Examples: Serial entrepreneurs who exited for $10M+

### Where to Find Angels

**Online Platforms:**
```
→ AngelList (angel.co) — largest startup investor platform globally
→ Republic (republic.co) — also allows crowdfunding
→ SeedInvest (seedinvest.com) — regulated equity crowdfunding
→ Gust (gust.com) — angel network for Africa and emerging markets
→ LinkedIn — search "angel investor [country]" directly
```

**African-Focused Angel Networks:**
```
→ Cairo Angels: cairoangels.com (Egypt, but invests pan-Africa)
→ Lagos Angel Network: lagosangels.com
→ Nairobi Business Angels: nbangels.co.ke
→ Ethiopian diaspora networks (LinkedIn, private WhatsApp groups)
→ ABAN (African Business Angel Network): africanbusinessangels.net
→ Venture Garden Group angel program (Nigeria)
```

**Events to Meet Angels:**
```
→ AfriLabs Summit: africalabs.net
→ Demo Africa: demo-africa.com
→ Seedstars World Competition: seedstars.com
→ Slush Africa (happens annually)
→ GITEX Africa (Marrakech): gitexafrica.com
→ eMerge Americas Africa Day
```

### Angel Investor Pitch Tips
```
→ Lead with the problem and your credibility (why YOU)
→ Show the product working — angels invest in real things
→ Know your numbers cold: CAC, LTV, MRR, growth rate
→ Keep the ask clear: "We're raising $100K on a $1.5M SAFE"
→ Follow up within 24 hours of any meeting
→ Ask for warm introductions to other angels they know
```

## Type 3 — Accelerators

### What They Are
Programs that provide:
- Funding (usually $50K–$500K)
- Mentorship and network
- Office space (sometimes)
- Demo day to investors
In exchange for: 5–15% equity

### Top Programs to Apply To (Priority Order)

**Tier 1 — Global (Hardest, Most Valuable)**
```
Y Combinator (YC)
  Website: ycombinator.com/apply
  Investment: $500,000 for ~7% equity
  Apply: 2× per year (January and July cohorts)
  Acceptance rate: ~2%
  Why apply: YC alumni have raised $600B+ combined. Network is unmatched.
  Location: San Francisco (3 months)
  Tip: Apply even if rejected. Apply again next batch.

Techstars
  Website: techstars.com
  Investment: $120,000 for 6% equity
  Multiple programs globally including Africa
  Acceptance rate: ~1%
```

**Tier 2 — Africa-Focused**
```
Founders Factory Africa
  Website: foundersfactoryafrica.com
  Investment: Up to $250,000
  Equity: 10–15%
  Focus: East and South Africa tech startups

Flat6Labs
  Website: flat6labs.com
  Programs in Cairo, Tunis, Accra, Bahrain
  Investment: $30,000–$70,000 for 5–10%
  Tip: Cairo program is most active

MEST (Meltwater Entrepreneurial School of Technology)
  Website: meltwater.org/en/mest
  Location: Accra, Ghana (but accepts East Africans)
  Investment: Salary during program + seed investment post-graduation
  Equity: Variable

Seedstars Africa

  Website: aws.amazon.com/activate
  Gives: AWS credits ($5,000–$100,000)

### Accelerator Application Tips
```
The video pitch (for YC):
  → 2 minutes maximum
  → Three things in the video: What you do, why you, traction/progress
  → Film with decent lighting and clear audio
  → Be direct — no buzzwords, no fluff

The written application:
  → Be specific. "We have 500 showrooms published" beats "we have traction"
  → Answer exactly what they ask — don't add extras
  → For YC: Their question "Why now?" is critical. Answer it with data.

Common rejection reasons:
  → Too early (no evidence people want this)
  → Team doesn't seem capable of executing
  → Market too small
  → Idea too derivative (copy of existing company)
```

## Type 4 — Seed Venture Capital Funds

### Who They Are
Professional investment firms that manage pools of capital from LPs (Limited Partners)
such as pension funds, family offices, and endowments. They invest in startups
in exchange for equity and expect a return of 10× or more within 7–10 years.

### How VCs Think
```
VCs need one portfolio company to return the entire fund:
  Fund size: $50M
  Goal: Return 3× = $150M
  If they invest $500K in 50 companies:
    49 companies fail or return modest amounts
    1 company must return $100M+ to make the fund work

What this means for you:
  → They need to believe YOUR company could be worth $100M–$1B+
  → Small, local businesses don't excite VCs
  → You must pitch the GLOBAL opportunity, not just Ethiopia
  → VCs cannot get excited about a company worth $20M — too small for their model
```

### How to Approach VCs
```
1. Never cold email — aim for warm introductions
   → Ask your angels to introduce you to VCs they know
   → Ask accelerator program managers for referrals
   → Use LinkedIn mutual connections

2. If you must cold email, make it impossible to ignore:
   Subject: "[Company] — $X ARR, YoY growth, raising $[Y]"
   Body: 3 sentences. Problem. Traction. Ask.

3. Research each VC before reaching out:
   → What have they invested in before?
   → Do they have Africa/your sector thesis?
   → Who is the specific partner to target?
   → Never send to "info@" — find the right person's name

4. Track every conversation in a CRM (even a simple spreadsheet):
   Investor name | Fund | Date contacted | Status | Notes | Next step
```

## Type 5 — Impact Investors & Grants

### Why Halehame Qualifies for Impact Investment
```
Halehame creates measurable social impact:
  → Democratizes business presence for SMBs with no digital access
  → Creates jobs and income for template developers (gig economy)
  → Enables financial inclusion through integrated wallet + payments
  → Serves markets traditionally excluded from global commerce platforms
  → SDGs aligned: SDG 8 (Decent Work), SDG 9 (Industry/Innovation), SDG 10 (Reduced Inequalities)
```

### Impact Investors to Target
```
Omidyar Network
  Website: omidyar.com
  Focus: Technology + social impact, emerging markets
  Investment size: Varies ($500K–$5M)
  Note: Very selective but strong brand signal

Acumen Fund
  Website: acumen.org
  Focus: Poverty-focused ventures
  Investment: Patient capital ($500K–$3M)
  Note: Long-term investor — 7–10 year horizon

Mastercard Foundation

IFC (International Finance Corporation — World Bank Group)
